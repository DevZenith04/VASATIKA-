# Home Loan by Sanjay Kumar Pandey — New Design Language

## Direction: Signal House

The website becomes a **signal house** for a complex financial decision: editorial, precise, human, and visibly engineered. It should feel closer to a premium architecture journal crossed with a modern fintech product than a conventional loan brochure.

The system uses a near-black ink field, warm bone paper, cobalt signal blue, acid chartreuse for active states, and a controlled coral for conversion actions. Headlines use **Space Grotesk** with tight, architectural rhythm; body copy uses **DM Sans**; numeric and metadata elements use **IBM Plex Mono**. The visual language is intentionally high-contrast and information-rich without becoming dashboard-like.

The layout grammar is asymmetrical. Heroes use a split editorial composition with oversized type, a photographic proof card, and a live numeric signal. Product pages use staggered modules, hard-edged media windows, thin blueprint rules, and horizontal journey rails rather than repeated rounded cards. Financial information is treated as a visual object: numbers are large, labels are monospaced, and disclaimers sit beside the result rather than being buried.

Signature motifs are the **signal line**, the **corner bracket**, and the **journey rail**. The signal line travels through sections as a fine cobalt rule with chartreuse checkpoints. Corner brackets frame images and form panels. The journey rail makes the PRD funnel—Discover, Calculate, Match, Compare, Apply—visible throughout the product.

Interactions should feel deliberate and tactile. Buttons snap upward with a short ease-out, sliders reveal a live numeric readout, forms progress in clear steps, and card hover states expose the next action rather than merely changing color. Motion is restrained, transform/opacity-only, staggered, and disabled for reduced-motion users.

Brand voice is direct, calm, and specific. Headlines name the decision; CTAs describe the next useful action. Examples: “Put the monthly number on the table.” and “Build a shortlist you can explain.” No generic filler or guaranteed-approval language is allowed.

The product remains customer-first: every decorative move must make a complex home-loan decision easier to scan, compare, or continue.
